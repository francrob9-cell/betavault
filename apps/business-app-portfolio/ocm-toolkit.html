Organizational change management

Sample OCM artifacts centered on what users actually say, feel, and do.

These filled examples use a fictional deal-operations platform rollout called AcquiFlow. The point is to show practical change management artifacts that measure sentiment, resistance language, adoption behavior, manager burden, workarounds, and readiness, not just whether a training invite went out.

Core measurement Resistance sentiment expressed through complaints, workarounds, repeated questions, peer language, and manager escalations.
Filled samples

OCM documents for a fictional platform rollout.

04

Manager Readiness Kit

Manager opening script

"The goal is not to monitor you more closely. The goal is to make the work visible once, in the right place, so you are not asked for the same update three different ways."

Escalation path

Workflow confusion goes to the change lead. Permission concerns go to system admin. Client-impact concerns go to the rollout owner within four business hours.

Response to frustration

"Tell me where the new step feels slower. We will either explain why the control matters or change the workflow if the control is not doing useful work."

Measured by manager confidence, fewer repeated explanations, and reduced emotional escalation in team meetings.

06

Workflow Walkthrough

  1. Scenario: A closing payment document is missing a signature block.
  2. User action: Coordinator opens the deal, tags the missing document, and routes to legal review.
  3. Control: The system requires a reason code before moving the item to client-ready.
  4. Language check: Ask the user to explain what happened without using project jargon.
  5. Observed friction: Users hesitate when choosing between "legal hold" and "client pending."

Measured by task completion, hesitation points, wrong-path clicks, and whether users narrate the workflow confidently.

10

Cutover Support Plan

  1. Day 0: Freeze spreadsheet edits at 4 PM and migrate open deals.
  2. Day 1: Live floor support, two open office hours, hourly issue triage.
  3. Day 2: Publish top five questions and update field labels that caused confusion.
  4. Day 3: Manager sentiment check: what are people saying when project staff are not present?
  5. Day 5: Executive update with adoption, complaint themes, client impact, and decisions needed.

Measured by support ticket themes, urgent escalations, unresolved blockers, and tone shift from "this is broken" to "how do I do this?"

12

Executive Narrative Brief

Week 3 summary

Adoption is improving, but the highest friction remains in finance operations. The resistance is not technical; users understand the buttons. The issue is perceived loss of speed and uncertainty about who can see payment comments.

Decision needed

Approve a simplified payment evidence queue label set and allow the rollout team to retire two spreadsheet exports after Friday's client service review.

Risk

If the language around payment controls is not clarified, users will continue using side-channel notes, weakening the audit trail the platform is meant to improve.

Measured by executive decision speed and whether leadership can describe adoption risk in operational language.
